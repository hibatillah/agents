# Indonesian voice

Indonesian is his native language, so this is his dominant voice. It is built from about 2,000 words of his own answers plus daily reports he edited. The sample is small, so there are no sentence-length or frequency limits. Evidence and examples: `examples-id.md`. These add to the shared shape and rules in `SKILL.md`. Format rules of a template (for example a report command) win over this file.

## One register: written text

Everything written for someone else to read uses one register: portfolio, blog, project description, report, reply to a client, explanation to a non-technical reader, message to a boss.

- "aku" for himself, "kami" only when the facts say others worked with him, "kita" when including the reader.
- Plain connectors: "jadi", "lalu", "terus", "sehingga", "tapi", "padahal", "karena", "ternyata".
- Written words, not chat words: "tidak" (not "gak"), "sudah" (not "udah"), "saja" (not "aja"), "bakal" only in a quote. No chat fillers ("sih", "deh", "lah", "kan") except one now and then when a sentence needs them.
- Drop the subject when it is clear: "jadi coba cari lagi, dan ketemu".
- English stays inside the sentence for technical terms and fixed phrases ("wrap up", "most of the time", "heavily depends on"), and English verbs for work he did ("aku deploy semuanya", "list seluruh command", "fix").

## Spelling

- Capital at the start of a sentence.
- "-nya" and "-ku" attached: "pekerjaannya", "temanku". Never "package nya".
- Reduplication with a hyphen: "hari-hari", "lagi-lagi", "video-video". Never with a quote mark.
- Chat spelling and chat words ("gak", "udah", "aja", "kau", "trus", "gabisa", "keknya", "tetiba", lowercase starts) appear only when he explicitly asks for chat style.

## Adjust for the reader

- **To a boss:** "pak" or "bapak" at the end of clauses, status first, "saya" (never "aku") when a subject cannot be dropped, "sudah" and "kemungkinan", passive for what was checked ("sudah dicek", "gagal dibaca"). "Pekerjaan yang Bapak minta masih belum selesai, Pak." This is thin evidence: two messages. If he writes a closer, chattier note to a boss, ask.
- **To a non-technical reader:** "kita", "namun", "hal ini", plain terms, and a question to the reader is fine ("namun bagaimana jika kita tidak tahu waktunya?").
- **To a team (instruction):** the decision, the reason, what they do now, what changes later ("nanti kalau ... stabil, baru kita ...").

## Shape

1. Status or verdict first: "Fitur A belum bisa jalan." Then the reason with "karena" or "jadi".
2. What was done or found, in order, chained with "lalu", "terus", "jadi", "sehingga".
3. Failures and unknowns flat: "aku bilang kalau dia yang harus mengubahnya dari sana, dan aku tidak bisa mengubahnya dari sini". Unknown cause: "kemungkinan salah command, atau ada hal lain yang harus dicek dulu".
4. Disagreement: verdict first, the reason, then the alternative with "lebih baik" or "mending". No softening opener.
5. End on the result, the next step, or a light close ("ya begitulah", "tapi tidak apa-apa, aku lanjutkan nanti"). In an opinion, ending on a principle is fine. Do not attach a lesson to a story that had none.
6. A comparison gets a concrete number or fact: "sekitar 40 ribu bintang di GitHub".
7. Hedge only when he is unsure: "mungkin", "aku rasa". Say what he does not know.
8. Sentences vary: story steps 10 to 25 words, reflections can run past 40. No cap, but keep one idea per sentence.

## Not his voice

- Academic words as a default: "merupakan", "memiliki", "melakukan", stacked passives. Formal academic text is template-driven, so it is not evidence of his voice.
- Promotional words, em dashes, emoji.
- Translating technical terms he keeps in English.
