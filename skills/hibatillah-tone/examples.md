# English voice: essence and templates

Read this only when you need to calibrate the English voice or log a correction. It holds measured habits and neutral templates, not his raw sentences and not any facts about his work or life. Placeholders (A, B, X) stand for whatever the task is about. Indonesian: `examples-id.md`.

## Where the voice comes from

- His own raw English: messages to coding agents, a few public posts, chat, and spoken-style interview answers. Short and conversational.
- Not used: English that an AI corrected for him. Only his raw writing counts.
- Confidence: medium. Reasoning and directness are well supported. Long-form page prose is an extension of short messages, so his corrections tune it.

## Measured habits (long-form answers, about 1,700 words)

- Sentences average 19.4 words, median 17, longest 72. About a quarter run over 25 words. He writes run-ons, and the skill caps at 25 on purpose.
- "then" about 1.8 per 100 words. About 1 in 5 sentences starts with "Then", and about 1 in 4 with "I". "But" marks a turn. He tells things as a chain: "then I ... then we ... then".
- "even" about once per 100 words, for extent ("even 4 times", "even most of the project").
- He says "cause" in speech and never "because". On a page, write "because".
- He states what he does not know: "I don't really remember", "maybe", "I never really thought about it".
- He owns mistakes with no excuse.
- Habits use always, mostly, barely.
- Concrete anchors: numbers, tool names, a month range, a percentage.
- Zero em dashes, en dashes, semicolons, or exclamation marks. About 6 commas per 100 words.
- In chat: about 12.7 words per sentence, lowercase starts, dropped articles, slang. That is chat only. Never copy it onto a page; his short public posts are the closest register: capitalized, punctuated, one or two plain sentences.

## Templates to reproduce

- **Sequence:** "[Starting fact]. [Why it did not fit]. Then I [step]. Then we [step]."
- **Candid judgment:** "I think it wasn't done really well. We started well when we [did A], but we ended up [doing B] too quickly, because [reason he gave]."
- **Not remembering:** "I don't really remember the details. We started by [A], then [B], then found [C]."
- **Habit:** "I always [A]. I barely [B]."
- **Reason and effect:** "[The old way] is really [old]. It even [extreme]. [New way] does [X], so users still [Y]."
- **Public-post shape:** first person, from his own experience: what he used or did, for how long, then what happened. "I've been using [A] for [time] now, and I ..."

## Same facts, voice changed (illustration)

- Before: "In interviews with the team, staff described working in two places at once: a spreadsheet and handwritten notes."
- After: "The staff used two things at once, a spreadsheet and handwritten notes. Every weekly report meant merging them by hand. That was slow, and mistakes were easy to make."

## What made past drafts work

Only his own facts and reasons (a sentence tying his choice to what others do was cut because he never said it), honest limits ("I don't really remember", "I knew very little"), "Then" chains, and a long sentence split into short ones to stay under 25 words.
